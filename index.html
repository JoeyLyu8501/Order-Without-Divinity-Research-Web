Preliminary Research

Muqarnas Beyond Divinity: From Sacred Geometry to Secular Spiritual Space

Research Position

From Sacred Ornament to a Vessel for Secular Contemplation

Muqarnas is a traditional architectural form composed of layered geometric units. It breaks continuous space into discrete honeycomb-like structures, producing a complex three-dimensional system of transition. Spatially, it mediates between square rooms and circular domes. Experientially, it transforms geometry, repetition, light, shadow, and depth into an abstract spiritual encounter, where sacredness is not depicted directly but perceived through spatial order.

Emerging around the tenth century in the Iran-Iraq region, muqarnas became increasingly systematized during the Seljuk period and later spread eastward and westward through the political and trade networks of the Islamic world. In modern contexts, however, its religious significance has often been weakened. In Soviet-era Uzbekistan, religious worship was systematically suppressed; today, traditional Islamic architectural elements are frequently de-sacralized and recast as national decorative language within modern public spaces.

This project asks what muqarnas can become when it is no longer centered on God. Rather than rejecting religious space, it translates the inner logic of muqarnas: geometric repetition, layered structure, endless order, and the tension of light moving across complex surfaces. These elements form a perceptual structure, a cognitive field shaped by repetition, layering, and light. It no longer serves theology, but becomes a framework for consciousness.

The goal is to create a de-religionized muqarnas, a spiritual vessel for the atheist. In this space, people no longer gaze upward toward the divine, but turn inward to confront and contemplate themselves. Geometry is redefined as both structural logic and sensory medium: calculated yet felt, ordered yet ambiguous. The space does not define spirituality; it creates the conditions for it.
